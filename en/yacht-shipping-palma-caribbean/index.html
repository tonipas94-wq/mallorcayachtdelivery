Quick answer

Yacht shipping means lifting the boat onto the deck of a cargo ship that carries it to another continent without it sailing under its own power. Ships leave Palma for the Caribbean and Florida between September and January and come back to discharge in Palma between February and June; the passage to Antigua or St. Thomas takes about 12 days. We bring the boat to the loading quay from any port in the Balearics, prepare it to the shipping line's instructions and handle the manoeuvre with the loadmaster. The owner books the freight with the shipping line.

How we work

Six steps, from booking to return

We coordinate dates with the shipping line so the boat reaches Palma within the loading window, not before and not after.

  1. Booking

    You book with the shipping line, or we help you request a quote. You send us the loading window.

  2. Technical data

    We gather the weight, dimensions and lifting drawings the shipping line asks for.

  3. Preparation

    We get the boat ready following the shipping line's list, at your berth or in Palma.

  4. Delivery

    We bring the boat to the loading quay in Palma within the agreed window.

  5. Loading

    We come alongside the ship and assist the loadmaster during lifting and cradling.

  6. Return

    In spring we collect the boat at discharge and take it to your berth.

Checklist

How a yacht is prepared for shipping

Every shipping line publishes its own requirements. These are the points we always check before taking the boat to the quay.

  • Real weight and dimensionsLength overall, beam, draft and air draft
  • Drawings with lifting pointsTo place the slings without damaging propellers, shafts or transducers
  • Antennas, radar and tall itemsLowered or removed if the shipping line asks
  • Covers, biminis and canvasRemoved and stowed inside
  • Loose items securedOn deck and inside
  • Fuel and waterAt the level the shipping line specifies; black water tanks empty
  • Batteries and systemsDisconnected or in the mode the shipping line specifies
  • Papers and keysCopy of the boat's documents on board and a labelled set of keys
Lift-on/lift-off or float-on/float-off

On most ships the boat is lifted by crane and set on a cradle (lift-on/lift-off). On semi-submersibles, the ship partly submerges and the boat motors in (float-on/float-off). Preparation and manoeuvre differ; we explain them for the ship you have booked.

FAQ

What owners ask about this route

Another question? Message us on WhatsApp and we will reply the same day.

When do yacht transport ships leave Palma for the Caribbean?

According to the schedules published by the shipping lines for 2026, sailings to the Caribbean and Florida load in Palma between September and January, at the end of the Caribbean hurricane season. Each sailing has a loading window of 10 to 15 days; the shipping line confirms the exact date a few days before.

How long does the ship take from Palma to the Caribbean?

About 12 days at sea to Antigua or St. Thomas, and about 5 more days to Fort Lauderdale (Florida). The return trip from the Caribbean to Palma takes about 14 days.

Who pays the freight and how much is it?

The owner books and pays the freight directly with the shipping line, which quotes it by the boat's dimensions (length, beam and height), weight and route. We invoice the delivery to the loading quay and the preparation and loading manoeuvre.

Do I need to be there for loading?

No. Our skipper brings the boat alongside the ship, follows the loadmaster's instructions and is present during lifting and cradling. We record the boat's condition with photos and video before and during loading and send them to you.

What needs to be prepared on the boat before loading?

Usually: lowering or removing antennas and protruding parts, taking down covers and canopies, securing anything loose, leaving fuel and water at the level the shipping line asks for, and providing drawings with lifting points, the real weight and dimensions. We take care of the physical preparation of the boat.

What about customs, VAT and insurance?

Taking a boat out of the European Union and bringing it back can have VAT and customs implications, and transport insurance depends on the contract with the shipping line and your own policy. Check with your adviser and the shipping line before booking; we provide any technical documentation they ask for.

Do you work with a specific shipping line?

We are independent: we work with whichever line you have booked, or help you request quotes from the ones calling at Palma. What matters to us is the date and the loading quay.

Do you also collect the boat when it comes back from the Caribbean?

Yes. When the ship discharges in Palma in spring, we collect the boat alongside, run a general check and deliver it to your berth in Mallorca, Ibiza, Formentera or Menorca.
